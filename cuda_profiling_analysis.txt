METHOD
Timing uses cudaEvent_t with cudaEventElapsedTime, NOT a host clock. CUDA kernel
launches are asynchronous, so a host-side timer around a launch measures only
the launch call and reports near-zero for the kernel. Events are recorded in the
stream and therefore bracket real device execution.

Five phases are measured:
    setup    host flattening + cudaMalloc + cudaMemcpy H2D + cudaMemset
    bomb     the bombardment kernel, accumulated over all storms
    relax    the relaxation kernel, accumulated
    reduce   the entire reduction while-loop, accumulated
    d2h      the two scalar cudaMemcpy device->host, accumulated

The host-side pointer swap between d_layer and d_layer_copy sits outside every
timing window: it is a pointer assignment on the host and would otherwise
contaminate the phase it was placed in.

SCOPE
Block size 256 was chosen because it is the value declared in the source on
every CUDA branch, and the only block size whose campaign data was never
affected by the grid_copy defect. The block-size timing comparison is already
covered by the measurement campaign (1410 rows over six block sizes); the
profiling exists to answer where the time goes, which the hybrid campaign showed
to be a question about workload, not configuration.

1.1 THE PHASE DECOMPOSITION RECONCILES
Unlike the MPI/OpenMP profiler, where four independent MPI_MAX reductions
produced a sum that corresponded to no real execution, the CUDA phases are
measured on a single device in a single stream. They add up:

    cuda-1D      A_1M   sum 0.144139 s   wall 0.144778 s   99.56% accounted
    cuda-shared  A_1M   sum 0.075054 s   wall 0.075676 s   99.18% accounted

The residual is the CUDA context initialization, argument parsing and storm file
reading performed before core() is entered, plus the final cudaFree calls. The
figures below may therefore be presented as a genuine decomposition of wall
time, and the percentages are percentages OF WALL TIME.

1.2 ACCOUNTING FALLS OFF ON THE SMALLEST WORKLOADS
    C_1k   cuda-1D 73.0% accounted   cuda-shared 72.5% accounted
    C_5k   cuda-1D 88.3% accounted   cuda-shared 83.0% accounted

At a wall time of 1.2 ms the fixed per-process costs outside core() are a large
fraction of the total. These two rows should be read qualitatively only.

The bombardment kernel accounts for 89-99% of runtime on every workload of

THIS IS THE SAME BOTTLENECK AS THE HYBRID IMPLEMENTATION
The MPI/OpenMP profiling campaign found bombardment at 97.7-99.98% of wall time
across 24 configurations. Two independently written implementations, on
completely different hardware, are limited by the same nested loop over cells
and particles. The conclusion is a property of the algorithm, not of either
parallelization strategy, and should be stated that way in the report.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. FINDING 2 - WHERE THE GPU STOPS PAYING OFF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Reading the table above from bottom to top shows the transition directly. As the
workload shrinks, the bombardment share collapses and the fixed setup cost takes
over:

    A_1M    bombardment 98.90%   setup  0.55%
    A_20k   bombardment 89.12%   setup  5.87%
    C_5k    bombardment 72.35%   setup 12.37%
    C_1k    bombardment 42.35%   setup 23.55%

On cuda-shared the effect is sharper still, because its bombardment is faster
and therefore a smaller share of an unchanged fixed cost:

    C_1k    bombardment 33.22%   setup 26.80%

At C_1k the GPU spends more time allocating device memory and copying the storm
data across PCIe than computing with it. The setup phase is essentially constant
per run - it depends on the number of particles, not on the layer size - so it
becomes dominant as soon as the compute shrinks.

This is the CUDA counterpart of the resolution-floor observation in the hybrid
campaign, and it is the honest answer to "when should this workload not be run
on a GPU".


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. FINDING 3 - WHAT SHARED MEMORY BOUGHT (AND WHAT IT COST)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 THE CONFOUND, AND WHY PROFILING WAS NECESSARY
cuda-shared differs from cuda-1D in TWO ways, not one:

    (a) the bombardment kernel loads particle data into __shared__ memory in
        tiles of BLOCK_SIZE, rather than reading d_flattened from global memory
        inside the inner loop;

    (b) cuda-1D pre-allocates the two ping-pong reduction buffers ONCE, outside
        the storm loop; cuda-shared still calls cudaMalloc and cudaFree INSIDE
        the reduction while-loop, on every pass of every storm. The
        pre-allocation optimization is present in the source only as a
        commented-out TODO.

End-to-end wall time cannot separate these. If cuda-shared were slower, wall
time alone could not say whether the tiling failed or the allocation overhead
swamped it. The phase breakdown separates them exactly: (a) lands in t_bomb,
(b) lands in t_reduce.

4.2 THE TILING GAIN - BOMBARDMENT KERNEL

    Test      bomb cuda-1D   bomb cuda-shared   Speedup
    ---------------------------------------------------
    B_3          0.023058           0.011538     2.00x
    B_10         0.076588           0.038263     2.00x
    A_1M         0.143188           0.074057     1.93x
    A_20k        0.007756           0.003936     1.97x
    A_100k       0.016935           0.008917     1.90x
    C_5k         0.002070           0.001110     1.86x
    C_1k         0.000554           0.000383     1.45x

A consistent 1.9-2.0x on every workload of meaningful size. The tiling replaces
repeated global-memory reads of d_flattened - performed by every thread, for
every particle - with one coalesced load per tile into shared memory, reused by
all threads in the block. The bombardment kernel was memory-bandwidth bound, and
the optimization addressed exactly that.

The smaller gain at C_1k (1.45x) is consistent: with only 1000 particles there
are few tiles, so there is little reuse to exploit.

4.3 THE ALLOCATION COST - REDUCTION PHASE

    Test      reduce cuda-1D   reduce cuda-shared   Penalty
    -------------------------------------------------------
    B_10           0.000128             0.000567     4.43x
    B_3            0.000051             0.000186     3.65x
    A_100k         0.000038             0.000109     2.87x
    C_5k           0.000029             0.000083     2.86x
    C_1k           0.000030             0.000077     2.57x
    A_20k          0.000033             0.000077     2.33x
    A_1M           0.000069             0.000127     1.84x

The penalty is real and behaves exactly as predicted: it scales with the number
of storms, because each storm runs the full reduction and therefore its own
sequence of cudaMalloc/cudaFree pairs. B_10, with ten storms, shows the largest
penalty at 4.43x; A_1M, with a single storm, the smallest at 1.84x.

4.4 WHY IT DOES NOT MATTER
The reduction is 0.17% of cuda-shared's A_1M wall time and 1.34% on B_10. A
4.43x penalty on a phase worth 1.3% of runtime costs about 1% of total time,
against a 2x gain on a phase worth 90%.

    Test      wall cuda-1D   wall cuda-shared   Speedup
    ---------------------------------------------------
    A_1M          0.144778           0.075676     1.91x
    B_10          0.080326           0.042457     1.89x
    B_3           0.024576           0.013186     1.86x
    A_100k        0.017870           0.009886     1.81x
    A_20k         0.008703           0.004906     1.77x
    C_5k          0.002861           0.001899     1.51x
    C_1k          0.001308           0.001153     1.13x

cuda-shared wins by 1.77-1.91x on every workload of meaningful size, DESPITE
carrying the slower reduction.

4.5 THE OPTIMIZATION STILL WORTH DOING
Porting cuda-1D's pre-allocated ping-pong buffers into cuda-shared would remove
the reduction penalty entirely. Expected gain: roughly 1% on B_10, less
elsewhere. It is the correct next optimization on that branch, and the profiling
data both identifies it and bounds its value - which is a better answer at the
defence than either "we did not notice" or "we optimized it without measuring".

6. METHODOLOGICAL NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 INSTRUMENTATION OVERHEAD
Each PROF_TOC performs a cudaEventSynchronize, which serializes the pipeline:
without instrumentation the kernel launches queue asynchronously and can overlap
with host work. With at most 10 storms and 5 phases the profiled binary performs
roughly 50 synchronizations per run.

The profiled wall times are therefore expected to be slightly HIGHER than the
campaign's unprofiled measurements. The phase PROPORTIONS are unaffected, which
is what this report relies on. No paired overhead measurement was taken; if the
report quotes absolute CUDA times, it should quote the campaign figures, not
these.

6.2 WHY NOT MPI_Wtime
The CUDA binary has no MPI context: energy_storms_cuda.cpp is compiled by nvcc
and linked without MPI. More fundamentally, kernel launches are asynchronous, so
any host-side clock around a launch measures the launch call rather than the
kernel. cudaEvent_t is the correct instrument.

6.3 OUTPUT CHANNEL CONSTRAINT
The CUDA branch of execute_once() in parallel_job.sbatch ends with 2>&1, unlike
the MPI branch which uses 2>&2. Both streams therefore reach suite_parse_time(),
which greps for "Time:" and takes the LAST match.

The profiler's machine-readable line is deliberately prefixed "PROFILE:" and no
line it emits contains the string "Time:". Had it printed, for example, "Total
Time:", that value would have silently replaced the real wall time in every
recorded row. This constraint must be preserved in any future edit, including
translation of the human-readable labels into English.

6.4 BUILD PROCEDURE
Profiled binaries were produced by copying cuda_core_profiler.cu over
energy_storms_cuda_core.cu inside a disposable git worktree on shared storage,
applying the same #ifndef BLOCK_SIZE guard the suite applies, then building the
standard energy_storms_cuda target with the unmodified Makefile. No branch was
modified and no commit was made by the job.

The order matters: the profiler is copied FIRST and the guard applied SECOND,
because the guard patches whatever file sits at that path. Reversing the order
would leave the profiler's own #define BLOCK_SIZE 256 unguarded and silently
override -DBLOCK_SIZE.

6.5 nvcc BUILDS ARE NOT BYTE-REPRODUCIBLE
The cuda-1D binary digest differs between jobs 983389 (fba429bf...) and 983390
(4d658628...) despite identical commit and flags, because nvcc embeds the
worktree path. For the CUDA section the md5 column identifies which build
produced a row, but two different digests do not imply two different programs.
The MPI/OpenMP binaries do not have this property.

6.6 CORRECTNESS
Every profiled run produced a Result payload; correctness was separately
established by the verification job of 2026-08-23 (70/70 matches across 7 tests
and 6 block sizes on both branches) and by the re-measurement campaign, which
records 60/60 PASS at every block size on both branches.

7. OPEN ITEMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Single repetition per cell. Sufficient for phase attribution; insufficient
     for statistical claims. The measurement campaign carries five repetitions.

  2. Block sizes other than 256 were not profiled. The reduction phase is the
     one whose behaviour would change most - the number of reduction passes is
     4 at bs32 against 2 at bs1024 for a 1M layer, and on cuda-shared each pass
     carries an allocation pair. Given that the reduction is under 2% of runtime
     at 256, this is unlikely to change any conclusion.

  3. cuda-first and main were not profiled. cuda-first carries a race condition
     in its 2D bombardment kernel and fails correctness at every block size; it
     is retained as evidence for the 2D->1D redesign, not as a measured variant.

  4. No paired profiled/unprofiled overhead measurement was taken (see 6.1).

  5. The custom-tests input directory remains untracked by git. It was found
     missing from the cluster three times on 2026-08-23; the cause was
     identified as the Mutagen sync pairing ~/Desktop/DuoWaves, which does not
     contain it, against the cluster copy. Eight of the twelve campaign tests
     depend on these files.
